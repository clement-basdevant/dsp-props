\newcommand{\rqOneText}{dsp-props detects \killFuncDsp{} of the \nFunctional{}
functional mutants and the ported suites \killFuncSop{}. Neither raises an
alarm on an equivalent mutant; among the ablations, only generic PBT does
(Section~\ref{sec:rq2}). The two sets of detected faults are not nested:
\killSopOnly{} mutants are detected only by the suites, \killDspOnly{} only by
dsp-props, and together they detect \killUnion{} of them.

Many of these detections owe little to the relations. With seed~0, dsp-props
detects \dspKillsSeedZero{} mutants. In \dspKillRelation{} of them a relation
is violated: \dspKillTolerance{} comparisons exceed their tolerance and
\dspKillAssert{} other checks fail, such as the transfer function or the DC
gain; these counts are the same \dspKillRelationStable. In the other
\dspKillOther, the mutant raises an exception, exceeds the time limit
(\dspKillTimeout{} mutants), or makes Hypothesis report several distinct
failures at once (\dspKillGroup{} mutants, which we count here because we did
not record what the individual failures were). Any test that calls the mutated
code on a valid input detects most of these. The classification oracle itself
raises or times out on \nFuncLoud{} of the \nFunctional{} functional mutants.
On the remaining \nFuncQuiet, which return a wrong value without raising,
dsp-props detects \killQuietDsp{} and the suites \killQuietSop.

Every miss of dsp-props involves operator parameters that no declaration uses. A
declaration fixes the coefficients of a filter or the rates of a resampler, and
dsp-props then varies only the input signal. Seven \code{lfilter} mutants break
filters of order 0 or 1, or filters whose numerator is shorter than the
denominator, and the declared filters have orders 4 and 2 with numerators at
least as long. The \code{upfirdn} survivors change the length of the full
output, which the declarations never see since they reach \code{upfirdn}
through \code{resample\_poly} and through a FIR filter truncated to the input
length, or ignore the last input sample, which every relation sees identically
on both of its sides. The suites miss other faults. Their only comparisons with
expected values on the FFT use length 30, which the subject computes with
Bluestein's algorithm; on powers of two they only check that \code{ifft} inverts
\code{fft}, which two mutants of the radix-2 twiddle factors still satisfy. They
also always pass the rates of \code{upfirdn} explicitly, so four mutants of its
default arguments survive.

On these subjects, dsp-props detects fewer faults than the ported suites. It
detects some faults that they miss, without expected values. Its misses point
to the choice of declarations rather than to the relations, but we did not
test whether additional declarations, such as a first-order filter, would
detect them.}

\newcommand{\rqTwoText}{On the generated mutants, the four randomised variants
detect almost the same faults (Table~\ref{tab:comparison}); counting a mutant
as detected when a majority of seeds detect it, they disagree on
\ablationDisagree{} functional mutants. The differences are between seeds: three FFT mutants are missed in some seeds by the variants with uniform
inputs and detected in every seed by dsp-props; two of them are the
twiddle-factor mutants that the ported suites miss. Generic PBT also raised a
false alarm in one of its \nSeeds{} seeds, on the correct FFT and on every FFT
mutant equivalent to it: Hypothesis drew a subnormal input of norm
$10^{-323}$, for which a purely relative tolerance is zero and a rounding error
of one subnormal fails Parseval's identity. The error model of dsp-props
includes an absolute floor, and neither dsp-props nor its other ablations
raised an alarm. Most generated mutants change the output by orders of
magnitude, and almost any input reveals them. The domain generators do not make
detection faster here: the median number of inputs before the first failure is
\firstMedDsp{} for dsp-props and \firstMedUni{} for uniform inputs (90th
percentile \firstPnnDsp{} and \firstPnnUni), and the median counterexample has
\cexMedDsp{} samples in both cases. They cost time, with a median of
\secMedDsp{}~s per mutant against \secMedUni{}~s for uniform inputs and
\secMedSop{}~s for a ported suite.

On the hand-written faults (Table~\ref{tab:hand}), every variant detects every
fault in every seed, except the fixed tolerance, which misses one resampler
fault in one seed. The variants differ in how fast they detect. With the domain
generators, the median failure appears at input \handDspFirstMed{} (90th
percentile \handDspFirstPnn{}); with uniform inputs at input \handUniFirstMed{}
(\handUniFirstPnn{}), and with generic PBT at input \handGenFirstMed{}
(\handGenFirstPnn{}). The 90th percentile of the counterexample length is
\handDspCexPnn{} samples for dsp-props, \handUniCexPnn{} with uniform inputs,
and \handFixCexPnn{} with a fixed tolerance. No variant raised a false alarm
on the correct references of this table. These faults were written by the
author of the relations and of the generators, which favours the generators.

In short, at 200 inputs per relation the generators and the error-model
tolerances bring no net gain in detection on either set of faults. Their
measurable effects are fewer inputs before a failure and shorter
counterexamples on the hand-written faults, a few seed-dependent misses
avoided on generated ones, and one false alarm avoided. They might matter
more with a smaller budget or on faults that need a specific input; we did not
measure this. One hand-written fault, a filter whose gain is wrong by
1\,\%, is detected only because its transfer function is declared: it is
still linear, time-invariant and causal.}

\newcommand{\conclusionResult}{On \nFunctional{} faults generated by mutation of
four small subjects, it detects \killFuncDsp{}, fewer than the \killFuncSop{} of
the ported test suites of NumPy and SciPy; the two detect partly different
faults and together detect \killUnion{} of them. More than half of its
detections come from the mutant raising an exception or hanging rather than
from a violated relation, and in our ablations neither the domain generators
nor the error models increased detection; they made failures appear after
fewer inputs, with smaller counterexamples, on hand-written faults. Its misses
involve operator parameters that no declaration exercised; the misses of the
suites, code paths that their few expected values do not reach.}
